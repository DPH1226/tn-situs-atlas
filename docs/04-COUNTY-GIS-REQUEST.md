# Wilson County GIS — request, not purchase order

**This replaces the six-layer $450 order.** Nothing on that list is needed to begin. What follows
asks for the two things that would genuinely improve the work, and asks for them the way a client's
contractor should.

> Comptroller's statewide weekly feed, but a copy direct from the County — with the County's own
> effective date on it — carries more evidentiary weight if a specific situs finding is later

> support of the County's own revenue review rather than as a data sale.

> **2. Boundary currency, which no file can tell us.** For Lebanon, Mt. Juliet and Watertown, we

> situs assignment that is correct against last year's boundary is wrong today.
>
> File Geodatabase is preferred over shapefile where available, as it preserves field names and
> types. If shapefile is what you have, that is fine.
>
> Happy to share what we have built so you can see how the County's data would be used.

## Notes for internal use — do not send

- **Do not open by asking for a fee waiver.** Ask for the layers as County work product. The fee
  provision at T.C.A. § 10-7-506(c) is triggered by a *request for a copy of a public record*; a
  county GIS department supporting a county finance review is internal data sharing, and the
  statute has no purchase on it. Framing it as a waiver invites the answer "we can't waive without
  a written policy."
- **If they quote the $75/layer price anyway**, that price is legally defensible and arguing about
  it is a losing position. Escalate to Aaron instead — it is his department's budget either way, and
  the circularity argument is his to make, not yours.
- **Never send a records request on Civvix letterhead.** OORC Advisory Opinion 15-01: corporations
  are not "citizens" and have no standing under the Tennessee Public Records Act. Any TPRA request
  must go out in the name of a named Tennessee-resident individual.
- **If you do end up buying**, cite OORC Advisory Opinion 09-02 and decline any licence agreement —
  a GIS custodian cannot condition the sale on one. That turns a recurring licence into a one-time
  purchase.
- **What not to ask for, and why:** zoning, water bodies, railroads, contours, subdivisions,
  commission districts, the thoroughfare plan, and the 2009 building footprints. The footprints in
  particular are 17 years old; Overture Maps publishes conflated building footprints refreshed
  monthly, free, under ODbL, and its August 2026 release is current.
