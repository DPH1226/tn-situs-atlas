# Pricing — anchored to ROI, replicable from Goodlettsville to Shelby County

## The unit of value

A situs correction is not a lead. Wilson County and its school department booked $61.8M in local
option sales tax last year across roughly 3,600 named businesses — about $17,000 per business, heavily
skewed toward retail and fuel. One cross-county correction therefore moves **on the order of $17,000
a year, every year, forever**, plus one year back. A single grocery or truck stop is $50–150K a year.
Civvix's TPP lead is priced at $10 because a lead is worth a few hundred to a few thousand dollars of
assessment, once. A packet-ready situs correction is worth fifty to a hundred times that, recurring.
The price should say so.

## The structure — three parts, each tied to a number the client already has

### Part A — Monitoring: basis points on prior-year local option collections, tiered

The base is the jurisdiction's own audited local option sales tax revenue (county plus school
department for a county; the city's own line for a city). No argument about the number, and it
scales with exactly what is at risk.

| Prior-year local option collections | Rate |
|---|---|
| first $25M | 15 bps (0.15%) |
| $25M – $100M | 10 bps |
| $100M – $500M | 5 bps |
| above $500M | 2.5 bps |
| **floor** | **$7,500** |

| Jurisdiction | Base (est. where marked) | Annual monitoring | Protected value / yr* | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goodlettsville (city) | ~$5M | $7,500 (floor) | $25–75K | 3–10× |
| Mt. Juliet (city) | ~$20M | $30,000 | $100–300K | 3–10× |
| Lebanon (city) | ~$26M | $38,500 | $130–390K | 3–10× |
| **Wilson County + schools** | **$61.8M** | **$74,300** | **$310–925K** | **4–12×** |
| Sumner County + schools | ~$80M | $92,500 | $400K–1.2M | 4–13× |
| Rutherford County + schools | ~$150M | $87,500 | $750K–2.2M | 9–25× |
| Knox County + schools | ~$200M | $112,500 | $1.0–3.0M | 9–27× |
| Shelby County + schools | ~$250M | $137,500 | $1.2–3.7M | 9–27× |

*0.5–1.5% of base — the range in the strategy memo, consistent with the Hendersonville calibration.
Bases marked ~ are estimates to be replaced with each jurisdiction's audited figure.

Monitoring includes: quarterly re-measurement of every rooftop against refreshed boundaries; the
roster join and adjudicated queue after each situs-report pull; both-directions reporting; the
quarterly bulletin; the calibration report; and the guarantee that nothing stays wrong for more than
the Department's one-year correction window. **The insurance framing is the right one:** a few basis
points on the protected base, in exchange for never losing a year.

### Part B — Reconciliation: $500 per adjudicated, packet-ready finding

The per-lead model, priced to what the unit is worth. Non-contingent and work-based: the county
decides what to submit and pays for the packet, not the outcome. Either direction. A first roster in
a county Wilson's size will likely produce 100–200 findings — $50–100K, once, against a year of back
money on every one.

Offer the contingency alternative in the same proposal — **12% of documented recovery for eight
quarters**, cheaper than HdL's 15% — and let procurement choose. Some jurisdictions can only buy
"free." Never both on the same finding.

### Part C — Annual business-license and TPP check: $10 per verified lead, unchanged

The existing Civvix product, run once a year against the same placed universe, bundled into the
engagement letter so it never needs its own procurement.

## What it does statewide

Tennessee's local option sales tax runs roughly $3B a year across counties and cities. With the
tiering, counties alone come to about **$3.5M a year at full penetration**, cities another **$2M**,
before a dollar of reconciliation or TPP work. At a realistic 35% of the state by year three: **~$2M
recurring plus findings**, on infrastructure that costs under $500 a month, because the surface is
statewide and a jurisdiction is a config file.

## Rules that travel with the structure

- **No contingency on monitoring, ever.** The fee is the fee; the jurisdiction keeps 100% of what
  comes back. This is the sentence that beats every competitor and neutralizes the predatory-vendor
  objection before it is raised.
- **County-and-cities package.** When a county signs, each city inside it monitors at 75% of its
  formula fee. It puts both sides of every intra-county seam inside one client family, which is how
  the bidirectional-findings problem stops being awkward and becomes a feature.
- **Multi-year price lock**, annual renewal, so a five-year relationship never trips an aggregation
  rule the county did not plan for.
- **Multi-county cities** (Goodlettsville, Portland, White House, Millersville, Ridgetop) are priced
  on their whole base and served from both county surfaces.
